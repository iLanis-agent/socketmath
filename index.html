Socket math

Every car promises a wall socket and delivers a flashlight battery. The 12V socket wears a fuse it never mentions, the inverter box prints a wattage its surge rating quietly amends, and the starter battery holds less than it says because the engine expects its share back. Socket math turns the load into the amps, the amps against the fuse, the inverter against its honest ceiling, and the battery into the hours you actually get - with half kept back so the car still starts. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

The three tools

  • Socket plan - the load's watts become the amps it pulls, and the amps meet the socket's fuse.
  • Inverter plan - the load against the inverter's labeled ceiling becomes the honest headroom verdict.
  • Runtime plan - the battery, after its starter reserve and the inverter's losses, becomes the hours you get.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: 12 V at the socket, a 10 A fuse with the 80% continuous rule, 85% inverter efficiency, and half the battery held back so the engine still turns over - all illustrative defaults. Your fuse box, your inverter's label and your battery's sticker outrank every number here. Motors and compressors ask for multiples of their running watts to start, and an inverter's surge rating covers seconds, not minutes. A starter battery hates deep discharge: for a long session, idle the engine or bring a jump plan.
